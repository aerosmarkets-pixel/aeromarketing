This is a different job to a landing page

Both matter and they are not interchangeable. Worth knowing which one you actually need before spending money on either.

01

A landing page catches paid traffic

One page, one offer, no menu, built to convert a stranger who clicked an ad thirty seconds ago. It is not trying to rank on Google and never will.

02

A website is what people find on their own

Someone searching your business name, someone checking you out after a referral, someone finding you organically months from now. It has to hold up to scrutiny.

03

The website is where trust gets decided

Real photos of your work, your service area, honest answers about pricing. A site that looks abandoned makes people assume the business is too.

04

Google needs somewhere to send people

Organic search and your Google Business profile both point at your site. If it is slow or thin, you rank below people who did the work.

When you don't need this

×

Your current site is fine

Loads fast, works on a phone, phone number visible, services clear. That is most of the job. A rebuild for the sake of a fresh look is not where your money should go.

×

You need leads this month

A website is a slow asset. It pays off over months. If you need the phone ringing in two weeks, run ads to a landing page instead and build this later.

×

You expect it to rank immediately

Building the site is the foundation, not the result. Local rankings take months and depend on reviews and consistency as much as the site itself.

×

You want a big brand experience

Animations and clever scroll effects do not book more jobs for a service business. Fast, clear, and easy to call beats impressive every time.
