Homepage versus landing page

Same visitor, same ad, same budget. The difference is what they land on.

Your homepage

Built for everyone

  • A menu with eight things to click
  • Every service listed, none emphasized
  • Contact form on a separate page
  • An About section nobody asked for
  • Written for people who already know you
  • Loads slowly because of the image slider
A landing page

Built for one job

  • No menu. Nothing to click but the form
  • One service, the one they searched for
  • Form visible without scrolling
  • Proof and answers to their actual objections
  • Written for a stranger who arrived ten seconds ago
  • Loads fast because that is most of the battle

How the page gets built

Not a template with your logo dropped in. Every page is built around one campaign and one offer.

01

Work out what they actually want

Someone clicking a gutter cleaning ad in October wants it done before the leaves finish falling. The page opens with that, not with your company history.

02

Write it before designing it

The words do the selling. Layout comes after the copy is right, which is the opposite of how most sites get made.

03

Build it around the form

Form above the fold on a phone, short enough that filling it in is not a chore, and repeated further down for the people who needed convincing first.

Phone screens first, always
04

Answer the objections on the page

Cost, timing, whether you are insured, what happens if they are not happy. Every unanswered question is a reason to close the tab.

05

Wire it to the follow-up

The form does not send an email nobody reads. It drops straight into the pipeline and triggers the text back within seconds.

06

Track everything

Pixel and conversion tracking installed and tested. Without it the ad platforms cannot learn who converts and you cannot tell what worked.

When you don't need one

×

Your site already has a strong service page

Clear offer, visible phone number, form near the top, loads fast on a phone. If that describes it, we run to it and save you the money.

×

You are testing whether ads work at all

If the budget is tight, spend it on traffic first. Learn whether there is demand, then build the page to catch it better.

×

You only run Google search

Search traffic is warmer and more forgiving. A decent existing service page can carry it. Meta traffic is colder and needs the dedicated page much more.

×

You want it to rank on Google

That is a different job. Landing pages convert paid traffic. If you want organic rankings you want a website build, which is a separate thing.
