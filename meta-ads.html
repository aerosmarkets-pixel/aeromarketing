Why this works for local service

Somebody scrolling Instagram at nine at night is not looking for a gutter cleaner. But their gutters are still full, and they have been putting it off for a month.

01

You reach people before they shop around

On Google, whoever they find is competing with four other tabs they already opened. On Meta you are the one who reminded them the problem exists. There is no comparison window because nobody else is in the conversation yet.

02

You can target a radius, not a keyword

Meta lets us draw a circle around the neighborhoods worth serving and stay inside it. No wasted spend showing your ad to somebody an hour outside your drive time.

03

The picture does the selling

Local service is visual work. A clean gutter next to a clogged one says more in half a second than any headline. Search ads are text. This is not.

04

It stays cheaper than search

You are not bidding against every competitor for the same handful of high intent keywords, so the cost to reach someone is usually lower. The tradeoff is they are colder, which is exactly what the follow-up system is for.

What actually gets built

Not a vague retainer. Here is the list.

[01]

Campaign structure

Built around your service area and budget rather than a template. Fewer, better funded campaigns beat a dozen starved ones every time.

[02]

Audience targeting

Radius, homeowner status, age range, and the interests that actually correlate with buying. Then we cut what is not converting.

[03]

Ad creative

Images and video built from your real work photos. Before and after, the job in progress, the finished result. Never stock.

[04]

Ad copy and hooks

Multiple angles per campaign so we can see which message your market responds to instead of guessing once and hoping.

[05]

Pixel and conversion tracking

Installed and tested before launch. Without it you are spending blind and Meta cannot optimize toward the people who actually book.

[06]

Weekly optimization

Kill what is losing, scale what is working, refresh creative before it burns out. This is the part that compounds.

[07]

Retargeting

The people who clicked and did not book are the warmest audience you have. Most agencies never build this.

[08]

Weekly reporting

Leads, cost per lead, and what changed. In a message you can read in a minute, not a dashboard you never open.

What a week looks like once it's running

Ads are not a set and forget purchase. This is the loop that runs every week you are a client.

01

Pull the numbers

Spend, leads, cost per lead, and which specific ads produced them. Broken out by campaign, not averaged into one useless number.

02

Cut what is losing

Ads spending without producing get turned off. No sentimentality about creative we liked making.

03

Push budget into what is working

The winners get more room. Done gradually, because moving budget too fast resets the learning and tanks performance.

Where most of the gain comes from
04

Check for creative fatigue

Every ad dies eventually. When frequency climbs and clicks fall, it is burned out and new creative goes in before results drop.

05

Send you the report

What happened, what changed, what is next. Plain English. You can ask why about any line of it.

This loop is the actual service. Anyone can launch a campaign once. The difference between an account that gets cheaper over time and one that quietly bleeds is whether somebody is doing this every single week.

When Meta ads are the wrong call

Worth saying out loud, because it saves us both a wasted month.

×

Your budget is under fifteen hundred a month

There is not enough data coming in to optimize against. You end up paying management fees on an account nobody can actually improve yet.

×

You cannot answer the phone

Meta leads are colder than search leads and they go quiet fast. If nobody is calling them back within the hour, we are buying leads for your competitor.

×

You are already at capacity

If you cannot take on more work, more leads is not the problem to solve. Raise your prices first, then come back.

×

You want it profitable in week one

The first two to three weeks are the algorithm learning who your buyer is. It usually gets cheaper after that. Going in expecting day one returns leads to killing campaigns right before they work.
