Why quiet leads are worth more than new ones

You already paid to get them. Getting them back costs nothing but a message that fires on its own.

01

They were interested enough to reach out

That is more than any cold audience you can buy. Something made them fill in a form. That intent does not vanish because they got busy on Tuesday.

02

Most people follow up once, then stop

One call, maybe one text, then it goes in the pile. The job usually goes to whoever was still there on the fourth touch, not whoever was fastest on the first.

03

Timing beats persuasion

Plenty of people are not ready this week and are ready next month. Automated follow-up means you are the name in front of them when that switch flips.

04

You cannot do this manually and you know it

Nobody running a service business is going to remember to text forty people on day nine. That is the entire argument for automating it.

What a sequence actually says

Real structure, real timing. Short, human, and it stops dead the moment they reply.

Minute 1

"Hey [name], got your request about the gutter cleaning. I'll give you a call shortly. If now is bad, just text me a better time."

Fires the second the form lands. Confirms a human has it, which stops them shopping around.

Day 1

"Tried you earlier and missed you. Happy to give you a rough number by text if that's easier than a call."

Removes the friction of a phone call. A lot of people prefer text and will engage here who would never pick up.

Day 3

"Still want that quote? No rush either way, just don't want to keep bugging you if you've sorted it."

Gives them an easy out, which is exactly why people answer it. Permission to say no gets more replies than pressure does.

Day 7

"Last one from me. If it becomes a priority later I'm easy to find. Good luck either way."

Closing the loop politely gets a surprising number of "actually, yes" replies. This is often the highest performing message in the whole sequence.

Day 30+

"Hey [name], we're booking up for the season. Thought I'd check in one more time in case the timing works better now."

The long game. Seasonal or situational nudge, months later, when their circumstances have changed.

Every one of these stops the instant they reply. The system hands the conversation to you and gets out of the way. Nobody gets a robotic message after they have already started talking to a human.

What gets built

[01]

New lead sequence

The one above. Runs on everyone who comes in and does not answer straight away.

[02]

Quote follow-up

For people you already priced who went quiet. Different messages, because they are further along.

[03]

Long term re-engagement

The month plus nudge for people whose timing was wrong rather than whose interest was wrong.

[04]

Past customer reactivation

For services people need again. A message at the right time of year beats buying a brand new lead.

[05]

Copy written for your niche

Not swapped placeholder text. What works for a roofer does not read right coming from a med spa.

[06]

Reply detection

Any response kills the sequence immediately. This is the piece most DIY setups get wrong and it is embarrassing when it fails.

[07]

Sending windows

Business hours only, never Sundays. Nobody wants a sales text at nine on a Sunday night and it makes you look desperate.

[08]

Opt-out handling

Compliant unsubscribe on every sequence. This is a legal requirement for automated texting, not an optional nicety.

When this is a bad idea

×

You have almost no leads yet

Nurture multiplies what you have. If you get three leads a month, fixing the top of the funnel matters far more than following up better.

×

You want it to sound like you wrote it personally

These are short and human but they are still templates. If you genuinely write bespoke follow-ups to every lead and enjoy it, keep doing that.

×

You do not have the CRM piece

Nurture runs inside the CRM. Without somewhere to hold the leads and detect replies there is nothing to build it on.

×

You want to message people who never contacted you

Not doing that. Automated texting to people who did not opt in is how numbers get shut down and how businesses get fined.
